# Building a reserve

The main goal of the first phase is to create a reserve of people who are familiar with the concept of a giftmoot economy and familiar with the benefits of a giftmoot economy.  The "job" of the reserve is to provide a passive support base when action in other phases is taken, and to provide a recruitment pool for that action, though it is likely that people will fall more within the former than the latter.

A reserve is necessary for any larger movement to work.  Without a reserve, political action is taken by a small, core group that looks isolated from the rest of society and can be rejected as insufficiently legitimate or representative of broader society.  The reserve places this political action into a more representative and legitimate context.

## Familiarity

A reserve of people familiar with the idea allows for general familiarity that makes the overall idea feel more normal.  At the very least, if political action is taken, the people in the reserve will say to themselves, "I know what they are doing, and I know why they are doing it."  The political actors are not alien or mysterious, which allows them to be more approachable and more likely to be heard.

It also allows the reserve to explain the motivations and goals of the political actors to others, should they ask, and the broader the reserve, the more likely it is that any person who sees political action and doesn't understand it will have a person they can ask to find out.  That more immediate degree of knowledge - that they can learn from a friend and not from a political elite or outspoken activist - will make the idea more palatable.

## Tacit consent

A reserve of people will also mean that if any political action is taken, they will understand _why_ it is being taken and be more likely to accept it as legitimate political action.  For example, if a new political party or social movement were to arise and a person was unfamiliar with it, they may not readily accept it as part of legitimate politics, and instead write it off as a temporary, inauthentic fringe movement.  The more they understand the movement and its goals, the more they are likely to agree that it makes sense people would advocate for such goals, and that there is no particular need to clamp down on that advocacy like there might be with some more extremist movement.

For example, a group that pops up out of nowhere and argues that money should be abolished might be written off as crazy, fringe, impractical and even dangerous, with the implication that they should not be listened to and perhaps that they should not be allowed to speak.  On the other hand, a group that emerges from a reserve of people familiar with the concept will more likely have a response where they are considered genuine political actors who have the right to speak and advocate.

## Tacit support

Further than accepting the political action as legitimate political action, a reserve of people is going to increase the chance that the action is supported, even if only passively.  A "reservist" at a family BBQ might say, "I understand why they're saying what they're saying" or "They have some sort of point, at least", and this can be taken as tacit support for the movement.  This can happen even if the person doesn't fully or clearly endorse the idea.

To some extent the function of tacit support is to create support where no individual person needs to clearly stand up and identify themselves as a supporter, but where a collective has a general support for the position.  For a lot of people, this is as much as they are willing or capable of doing in regards to political action, in part because the political context that individuals find themselves in are not pure ideological circumstances but intersections of lived experiences.

## Recruitment pool

Of course, reservists also act as a potential recruitment pool, with people who are willing to go further and take political action emerging from the group that is familiar with the concept in the first place.  This not only adds to the potential number of people who are going to do the bulk of the frontline work, but also adds personal connections between those people and the reserve pool that they are drawn from, making the movement tangible and personal to many of the reservists.
