# How we get there

It is one thing to know where you are going, and another thing to know how to get there.  In my critique of liberal market economies and proposal of a giftmoot economy, I have described where I think we should be going.  The next question is, How do we get there?

There is a big and a small part to this.  The small part is relatively straightforward: building awareness, proposing action and policies.  While I don't know exactly what those steps should look like, the basic idea of them is intuitive.  The big part is harder: how to overcome the opposition of very wealthy people.  The two are probably not all that distinct, but both need to be attended to if there is any real intention of success.

Any sort of large social change requires a lot of people, and I in no way imagine that I am capable of developing a strategy on my own that will suffice for a movement with a lot of people.  So a major factor in any movement is collaboration.

That said, here is the approach that I imagine, which I've separated into three vague phases: pre-imagined society, prefigurative society, and transitional society.  In the first the goal is to build awareness, familiarity, and plausibility.  In the second, the goal is to have people engage in some level of behaving and thinking of the society they want.  In the last step, specific policies and actions would put the new economic model in place - first with prepatory and stop-gap measures, then with motivating policies, and finally with full implementation.  I'll describe the three phases here roughly, and then flesh them out in more detail in the next few articles.

## Pre-imagined society

One of the biggest hurdles for an new paradigm is making it plausible to people - radical change is, in many cases, unimaginable.  Something that is radically different doesn't have enough practical or apprehensible things for a person to cling on to.  If an idea is vague and idealistic, then people have some trouble taking it seriously - "That sounds nice in _theory_," they say, "but I'm not sure it would ever work in _practice_."

The goal of the first phase, then, should be to make this vague and idealistic model something that is specific and apprehensible, so that a person can begin to imagine what it would be like to be in such a society.  This involves a few steps: building awareness of the idea, building familiarity of the idea, getting them to imagine living within the idea, and producing lots of specific details that they can grab onto.  The outcome should be that people can walk about current society and have a confident and intuitive understanding about what would be different in the giftmoot economy - and, ideally, practically desire those differences.

Some practical steps in this phase are to make awareness campaigns with posters, social media, podcasts, and the like, and also to ask people to do two pre-imaginative exercises: spend some time in their daily life trying to identify where gift-giving happens already in the economy wherever they come into contact with it; and two, spend some time in their daily life to try and identify how an economic interaction they are involved in would be different in a giftmoot economy.  Ideally, people could send in these thoughts to create a catalogue of specific examples.

Once people are familiar with the ideas, there will be a reserve of people who will have a more positive response to any action taken toward that society - they will be familiar why the action is being taken, what it should lead to, and why they might benefit from it.

## Pre-figurative society

The next phase is to begin some level of everyday practical action.  The goal here is twofold: first, that there might be immediate benefits, and second, that contact with some level of reality with the model will make it more likely people will accept and engage with any action taken towards it.  That is, if there is more gift-giving integrated into society, people will be more likely to understand the benefits and support the advancement of more.

The primary thing to encourage here is the development of communities whose members mutually recognise the idea and communities that begin to put it into practice.  I would suggest that there are three likely stages here: a talking and listening stage; a talking, listening and acting stage; and a grassroots organisation and implementation stage.  The first stage would create "places of listening", where people gather to share the results of their pre-imaginative exercises.  Not only would this give people the chance to discover more examples and discuss them in more detail, but it would also give people the chance to build community connections with people who might share some perspectives with them.

The second stage would be to hold some assemblies that conduct demotherapeia - a type of grassroots "democracy as therapy".  This is a type of non-adversarial democratic forum where people engage in truth-telling, questioning and reflection, and then have the opportunity to make personal commitments.  The idea would be that through these commitments people would begin to engage in more gift-giving within their local communities.

The third stage would be to form a rudimentary giftmoot.  These giftmoots would obviously exist within an exchange economy and therefore would be limited in the way in which they would be able to operate compared to a full giftmoot economy, but establishing an organisation with formality, regularity and legitimacy would start to entrench the idea, and the benefits would not just be immediately beneficial but also provide familiarity and advocacy.

## Transitional society

The previous two phases can be done, theoretically, with no support from formal political institutions, but full change would require changes to the regulatory environment of the economy, and that would require coordinated and institutional political action, such as pressure on government parties, the formation of political parties, lobby groups, or the like.

In this space, it is possible to outline some of the policy changes that could assist in moving from an exchange economy to a giftmoot economy, but the organisational details of how to achieve that I will at this point leave up to further collaboration.  I would suggest that there are three types of policies to implement, though the categorisation is not always very strict:

#### Immediate impact and stop-gap policies

These are policies that aim to expand gift-giving to help people in need right now, before any new economic model is implemented.  That might include things like increased subsidies for medicine and goods, including housing, increased welfare, and so on.

#### Prepatory policies

These are policies that help to create the framework of a giftmoot economy, such as establishing giftmoots as recognised institutions and providing funding for them, providing essentials or universal basic income in order to allow employee mobility, and considering tax breaks and transaction taxes to motivate businesses towards gift-giving behaviour.

#### Implementation policies

These would be policies that transition from prepatory policies to a full giftmoot economy, including buffering the transition to assist businesses, and creating an interface between a giftmoot economy and other market economies by making trade and investment attractive, as well as policies to expand gifting networks into other economies.
